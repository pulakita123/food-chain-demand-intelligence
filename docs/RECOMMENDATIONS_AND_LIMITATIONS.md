# Recommendations and Limitations

## Recommendations

### 1. Plan service coverage around demand

The order timestamps show strong activity at noon, 13:00 and early evening. The restaurant could compare these patterns with staffing rosters, preparation times and queue lengths before adjusting shift coverage.

### 2. Monitor popular products

The dashboard identifies The Classic Deluxe Pizza as the leading pizza by quantity. The team should monitor its ingredient availability and stock levels, while also tracking substitutions and out-of-stock events.

### 3. Review quantity and revenue together

The Thai Chicken Pizza leads the dashboard's revenue comparison, while the Classic Deluxe leads by quantity. This is a good reason to monitor both sales volume and revenue rather than using one ranking as the only measure of product success.

### 4. Use category and size mix for inventory planning

Category and size analysis can help estimate demand for different product ingredients and packaging sizes. A more complete forecast would also need inventory records, waste and supplier lead times.

### 5. Investigate low-performing products with more context

A product with lower sales may still be strategically important or have a strong margin. Before changing the menu, the business should consider cost, preparation time, customer feedback and repeat-purchase behaviour.

## Limitations

The available files include order dates/times, product types, categories, sizes, quantities and listed prices. They do not include ingredient or product cost, labour and delivery expenses, discount and coupon information, refunds, customer identifiers, marketing campaign data or customer ratings.

For this reason, this project analyses sales and demand patterns. It does not calculate profit, customer lifetime value or causal effects of promotions.

## Future enhancements

If additional data were available, I would:
1. calculate gross margin by pizza type and size;
2. analyse customer retention and repeat-order behaviour;
3. compare weekday, weekend and seasonal patterns;
4. forecast demand by product and hour;
5. compare sales patterns with stock-outs and food waste; and
6. build an operational view combining demand, staffing and preparation capacity.
