# Business Problem & Analytical Objectives

## Business context

A pizza shop receives orders throughout the day and sells products that differ by type, size and price. A total-sales figure is useful, but it does not explain what customers are ordering, when demand is strongest or which products contribute most to revenue.

A sales manager needs a view that connects order activity with product performance. This can help the team plan inventory, staffing, product availability and promotions.

## Problem statement

The objective of this project is to analyse a year of pizza-shop transactions and identify the products, categories, sizes and time periods that shape sales performance.

I approached the work as an analyst supporting a restaurant manager: first establish the overall sales picture, then investigate the patterns behind it, and finally translate those patterns into practical questions for the business.

## Business questions

1. How many distinct orders were placed?
2. What was the total sales revenue?
3. Which pizza and size combination has the highest listed price?
4. Which size is ordered most frequently?
5. Which pizza types lead by quantity?
6. How does quantity sold differ by category?
7. Which hours have the highest order volume?
8. How many pizza types are available in each category?
9. What is the average number of pizzas sold on an active sales day?
10. Which pizza types generate the highest revenue?
11. What percentage of revenue does each pizza type contribute?
12. How does cumulative revenue build over time?
13. Which three pizza types generate the most revenue within each category?

## Intended users

- Restaurant owners and general managers
- Sales and operations managers
- Inventory and purchasing teams
- Analysts preparing sales-performance reports

## Scope

**Included:** orders, order timestamps, order-line quantities, pizza types, sizes, list prices, categories and ingredients.

**Not included:** cost of ingredients, labour, delivery expenses, discounts, refunds, customer IDs, customer feedback or marketing spend.

## Decisions this analysis can inform

The results can help the business identify peak ordering hours, monitor popular products, compare category and size mix, and decide which products deserve closer review.

The dataset does not contain cost or customer-level information, so it cannot support claims about profitability, customer retention or the causes of product performance.
